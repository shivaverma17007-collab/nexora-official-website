1. About this draft

This draft is for the NEXORA public website and its productivity and self-development app. The current app feature areas described publicly are tasks, study, routines, trackers, journaling and mindful breathing. The website does not represent planned user-profile or community functionality as already available.

The website package itself does not include an analytics SDK, advertising pixel, embedded marketing tracker or website data-entry form. Its contact link opens the visitor's own email application. This describes the website code in this package only; it is not a statement about technical logs generated by the hosting provider or about data handled inside the app.

2. Information the app may handle — verify before publishing

The responsible operator must inspect the deployed app, database, authentication configuration and connected services, then replace this section with a precise description. Do not assume every item below is collected; this is a verification list, not a declaration that the app currently collects it.

  • Account and sign-in information, such as email address, phone number, provider identifiers or authentication events, if account features are enabled.
  • Information a person enters into tasks, study tools, routines, trackers or journal entries.
  • Preferences, settings, timestamps, usage events or device and technical information, if generated or stored.
  • Information associated with breathing or mindful-practice sessions, if any such information is saved.
  • Support messages that a person chooses to send to the public contact email.

Pay particular attention to journal entries and other free-text content, which may contain sensitive personal details entered voluntarily by a user. State whether such content is stored, where it is stored, who can access it and how it can be deleted.

3. Why information is used

Before this policy is finalised, the operator must document the actual purposes for each category of information. Potential purposes to verify may include providing app functionality, maintaining user preferences, authenticating accounts, responding to support requests, protecting the service from misuse and meeting legal obligations. Remove any purpose that does not apply, and do not add a purpose unless it is genuinely used.

4. Storage, providers and sharing

Identify every service that receives or stores app information. The app's current technical configuration must be checked, including whether Supabase is used for authentication or database services, which data is sent to it, the relevant region and applicable provider terms. Also identify the website hosting provider and any email, authentication, backup or other integrations actually in use.

Do not publish a promise that information is never shared, never transferred or stored only in one country unless that statement has been technically and legally verified. List the relevant providers, their roles and links to their privacy information in the final version.

5. Retention and deletion

The final policy must explain how long each category of information is kept, what happens when an account or item is deleted, whether backups remain for a period, and how a person can request deletion. These details must match the app's actual database, backup and provider configuration. No retention period is asserted in this draft.

6. Security

The operator should describe security practices only after confirming that they are implemented and maintained. Do not interpret this draft as a guarantee that data is completely secure or inaccessible to unauthorised parties. Any final security statement should be accurate, appropriately limited and reviewed.

7. Children and age-related use

The intended age range and any rules for younger users must be decided and stated by the operator. Before publishing, check whether the app is directed to children, whether parental consent or additional safeguards are legally required, and how requests concerning a child's information are handled. This draft does not set an age threshold.

When a final policy is published, show its effective date and explain how material changes will be communicated. Update the policy when the app's features, data flows, providers or legal obligations change.
